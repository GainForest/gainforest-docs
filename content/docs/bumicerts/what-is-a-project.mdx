<Callout type="success">
A **Project** is the main page for your environmental work on Bumicerts. It brings together your story, goals, updates, and evidence in one place.
</Callout>

<ButtonLink href="https://gainforest.app/projects" variant="primary">Explore Projects</ButtonLink>

A Project can represent work such as forest restoration, biodiversity monitoring, rewilding, conservation data collection, acoustic monitoring, community stewardship, or another nature-based effort.

## What a Project does

<Cards>
  <Card title="📖 Tells your story">Explain who you are, what you are doing, where the work happens, and why it matters.</Card>
  <Card title="🔎 Holds your evidence">Link observations, sounds, tree data, files, reports, and external links that support your work.</Card>
  <Card title="📣 Builds visibility">Share your Project with funders, partners, reviewers, community members, and other supporters.</Card>
  <Card title="📊 Supports reporting">Use your Project as a record for stewardship, impact reporting, grants, and funding conversations.</Card>
</Cards>

## What a Project can include

A Project can bring together:

* the story and purpose of the work
* who is leading or contributing to it
* where the work is happening
* project goals and updates
* biodiversity observations
* photos and field notes
* bioacoustic recordings
* tree data
* drone imagery
* files, reports, dashboards, or external evidence links
* other evidence reviewed or supported by the GainForest team

## Creating a Project

When creating a Project, start with a clear name and a short summary that people can understand quickly.

<Figure src="/assets/project-name-summary.webp" alt="Project creation screen for name and short summary" caption="Use a clear Project name and a short summary." />

Then explain the Project story: what work is being done, what methods are used, what evidence exists, and what is changing over time.

<Figure src="/assets/project-story.webp" alt="Project creation screen for project story" caption="The Project story helps people understand the work, methods, and evidence." />

You can also choose focus areas that describe the type of work, such as nature monitoring, reforestation, forest protection, or community stewardship.

<Figure src="/assets/project-focus.webp" alt="Project creation screen for focus areas" caption="Focus areas help people understand what kind of work the Project represents." />

When the Project is created, it becomes ready to share and receive support.

<Figure src="/assets/project-created.webp" alt="Project created confirmation screen" caption="Once created, your Project can be shared and updated with evidence." />

## Projects and evidence

Data uploads can be linked to a Project as supporting evidence. This helps others understand what happened, when it happened, where it happened, and what data supports the Project’s story.

For example, a Project might include:

* biodiversity observations from a weekly BioBlitz
* bioacoustic recordings from an AudioMoth deployment
* tree data showing planted, regenerated, or existing trees
* drone imagery reviewed through the batch upload process
* files, reports, dashboards, or external evidence links

## Why Projects matter

Projects make environmental work easier to:

* explain to new audiences
* organize over time
* connect with evidence
* report impact
* gain visibility
* attract funding and partnerships
* use in opportunities such as grants or challenges
